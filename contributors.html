The people behind this

A safety idea only becomes real when umpires, players, clubs and leagues are willing to try it in a live game. Everyone below helped move it from a drawing to a wicket.

Created by

Aashi AnupVandeventer Middle School, Frisco, Texas, USACo creator
Anish AnupLiberty High School, Frisco, Texas, USACo creator

What began as an experiment in the backyard turned into an end to end engineering project: sketching the design, building prototypes and deliberately breaking them, testing through live matches, counting dismissals across hundreds of scorecards, and publishing all of it openly so anyone can build on it.

The part we did not expect was how much of it depended on people. Convincing a league to back something unproven. Listening to umpires explain why a part that costs more earns its place: a second stopper stops the bail guard collar sliding all the way to the ground, so after each stumping attempt the umpire lifts the collar back up a few inches instead of bending down to pick it up off the pitch. A few seconds each time, over a whole day in the field. Learning from coaches who had watched thousands of dismissals and knew things no measurement would have told us. Answering the same question from players until the answer was clear, and from organizers who had to make it work across dozens of grounds.

And a great many hours in the aisles of Lowe’s, Home Depot and Ace Hardware, and deep in Amazon listings, describing the idea to whoever would listen so they could point us at a part we had never heard of, then buying one or two at a time and testing them properly to see what survived a cricket ball.

A design only becomes real when the people who use it are willing to help you fix it, and working alongside them turned out to be as much a part of the engineering as anything on the drawing board.

It has been hard work and a great deal of fun, and we have learned more doing this than from anything else we have tried.

Leagues and clubs

Dallas Cricket LeagueThe world’s first league to officially adopt bail guards, for its Fall 2026 season. 201 teams, more than 6,900 players. Volunteers led by Mr Kuljit Singh Nijjar worked tirelessly to make it happen at that scale: studying the design, putting out flyers, answering questions from thousands of players, packaging and distributing the guards across several distribution days, and staying on call through the opening games.Publicly backed
North Texas Cricket Association (NTCA)Encouraged this initiative and raised awareness across the association, including at the NTCA banquet in February 2026. Offered valuable suggestions for testing, adopted bail guards for the NTCA President’s Cup, and encouraged their use across other NTCA matches.Adopted and championed
Grand Prairie Cricket ClubLed by Mr Faisal Akthar. Fitted bail guards on both Grand Prairie grounds and ran the GPCC Winter Cup with them on every wicket. GPCC volunteers also filmed every ball, from several angles and in excellent quality. That coverage is how the cricket community first saw the device work in real matches.Used in games
All Stars Frisco Cricket AcademyVolunteers, led by Mr Adi Kattari, made the devices used in the GPCC Winter Cup and some of the USA Hub Spring 2026 matches involving All Stars teams. Early support, and some of the very first trials.Early trials
USA Hub, DallasEncouraged the initiative from early on, gathered feedback through practice sessions and matches, and moved to adoption for the 2026 fall league.Adopting

Players and team matesPlayed the games that produced every number on this site, encouraged the idea when it was still unproven, and offered suggestions from the middle that no drawing could have given us.Field trials
CoachesBacked the idea early, offered technical suggestions from years on the field, and used bail guards in practice and in matches so more players saw them working.Field trials
ScorersRecorded the scorecards the field trial data is counted from.Records
Friends and well wishersSuggestions that improved the design, and the steady encouragement that keeps a volunteer project moving through the slow stretches.Support

Umpires, players, team mates, coaches, scorers, volunteers, friends, well wishers and family have all signed and shared the petition. A safety idea only travels as far as the people willing to vouch for it, and that support has meant a great deal.
